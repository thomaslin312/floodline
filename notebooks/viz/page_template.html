Mark by mark

Why a constant fails

HAND measures each floodplain cell against its nearest drainage, which across 491 km² is usually a small tributary rather than the main stem. The threshold that would place the water correctly ranges from −0.19 m to 12.32 m across these 16 marks, so no single value fits. Fitting the best possible constant still misses every mark by more than a metre.

Finer data made it worse

At 30 m the channel bed at the gauge reads 4.81 m NAVD88; at 10 m it reads 0.89 m, because 10 m resolves the channel. stage − bed therefore grew from 7.97 m to 11.89 m and the constant model flooded deeper. Resolution only helps once the stage conversion is right.

The improvement cost recall

Ten of sixteen marks fall inside the rating-curve extent, against sixteen of sixteen before. The constant model "hit" every mark by flooding 98% of the watershed. That is why hit rate alone is a useless metric, and why bias belongs beside it.

What this is not

A screening model, not a hydraulic one. Manning's equation assumes steady uniform flow, the cross-sections come from a DEM rather than a survey, roughness is one guess per reach, and one gauge's discharge is spread to ungauged reaches by drainage-area ratio — so a storm that hit one tributary and missed another is not represented.
